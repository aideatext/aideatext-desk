1 PDF → Markdown free and complete

Your document —thesis, report, case file, manual— in a file that ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini read whole and properly structured, with headings, lists and tables where they belong. Free, no page cap, and it never leaves your browser.

Why convert it instead of pasting the PDF?

A PDF describes how a page looks —the position of every character, the font, the coordinates—, not the logical structure of its content; Markdown does preserve the hierarchy: headings, lists, tables, emphasis. That makes the content processable, not merely visible. A badly extracted PDF arrives with absurd line breaks, columns jumbled together, repeated headers and footers and text in the wrong order, and that degrades the quality of any analysis you run afterwards with ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. It is a quality problem, not a size problem.

4 Document analysis with semantic reasoning graphs done by a person

Your document is drawn as a graph, and the shape of the drawing is the reasoning holding it together. It is the AIdeaText technique, and here you learn to read it on your own text.

That means we do read what you wrote: the three columns above never touch your file; this one does. In one or several sessions we cover:

What reasoning holds your argument together?

You see it in the shape. A star is deductive: one concept holds up the rest. A funnel is inductive: loose cases at the bottom that a few ideas gather at the top. Crowns, abductive: the concepts the explanation passes through. Two lobes, analogical: two developments in parallel and the lines crossing between them.

Which concept carries the most weight?

Colour tells you: it measures how many other concepts it connects to, and that is the weight that matters in an argument. Careful with size: size is frequency, not importance —a repeated filler word comes out large—.

Is every concept connected?

Islands are visible at a glance. Distance from the centre is not decorative: it is how many steps each concept sits from the core. A concept nobody touches is an idea you stated and never wove in.

What sets it apart: the graph is not laid out by a simulation, so a concept only moves when what it measures changes —compare two drafts and the movement is the diagnosis—. It does not say whether the reasoning is sound: it says how it is built.

How it works

Your PDF is opened with PDF.js inside your browser. AIDesk walks every page, measures how much extractable text it has and decides whether it is text, a scan or a blank page. With the text pages it assembles a Markdown file with one heading per page and hands it to you as a local download. There is no server anywhere in that path: no request uploads your file. The whole document is converted; there is no page cap.

The measurement box compares the characters of the text extracted from the PDF —layout noise included— against those of the Markdown produced, estimating one token per 4 characters. It is the result for your file, not an average or a promise. The Markdown often comes out the same size or slightly larger, and the screen says so in those words: what you gain here is structure, not token savings.

Security

It uploads nothing

We do not receive your document, and we could not even if we wanted to: this site declares a security policy (connect-src 'self') that forbids the browser from sending data to any other server.

Do not take our word for it. Check:

The one thing we do send

When a conversion finishes, your browser adds 1 to a public counter. It goes without your file, without your name and without your IP: it is literally a +1, with an empty request body. You can see it in the Network tab, next to the absence of any upload.

How to check it yourself

  1. Open the developer tools (F12), go to the Network tab and convert a PDF. You will not see a single upload request.
  2. Press the button above: we will try to send data to an external server in front of you, and you will watch the browser stop it. The violation is written to your console by the browser, not by us.
